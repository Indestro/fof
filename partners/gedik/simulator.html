Two findings that argue against our own model. (1) Tiering hurt — 1.51x vs 1.60x equal weight. The best 2024 books were Wischoff (2.40x) and Quiet (2.66x); JAM, our strongest prior-vintage name, sits at 0.96x on five positions. Weights fitted to one period misfire on the next: start near equal weight, re-tier at Fund II. (2) Unskilled co-invest hurt too — 12 positions drawn at random from the same companies return ~1.27x median even with no fee layer, pulling the portfolio to 1.49x. The simulator credits co-invest with informed selection; this window cannot prove that skill, so treat the premium as an assumption.

Model notes & limitations What is modelled. Return distributions are calibrated to public VC benchmarks; the selection effect to our own conversion measurements; model internals are withheld. Benchmark rows are public-summary approximations (Cambridge Associates-style US vintage stats; the Kauffman 2012 standard — we test only its ≥2.0x-net leg, not its public-market-equivalent leg). Cash flows assume 3-year calls, distributions in years 5–10 (60% of value) and residual NAV at year 10; our IRR is a fully-realised 10-year cash IRR, harsher than the interim NAV-based IRRs benchmarks quote.

Where the numbers are assumptions. Per-manager premiums are measured where the denominator is known (JAM: 82 positions, 6 unicorns) and explicit assumptions where it is not (Mei Zuo: 4 verified unicorn positions, book size unverified — set to a ~30-check mid case). The co-invest sleeve assumes precursor-informed selection, no manager fee layer (+25%), single-company dispersion, and allocations contingent on real LP commitments — the 2024 window cannot prove that selection skill.

Reading the outputs. Adding managers raises the mean but widens dispersion, so the median LP outcome can fall while the left tail fattens — compare the mean shown beside the median. Size cuts both ways: a scale penalty above (~9% per doubling) and, below, LP check minimums ($2M star anchors, $0.75M emerging) plus ~$250K/yr fixed operating cost. Backtest marks are dilution-adjusted estimates from public round data, not audited returns.
