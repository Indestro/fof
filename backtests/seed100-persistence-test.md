# Does the BI Seed 100 predict anything? A persistence test (2026-08-16)
Question: would a reputation ranking improve manager selection for the FoF?
Cheapest decisive test: if the ranking measures skill, membership should persist year to year —
skill does not change much in 12 months. If it measures recent luck, it should churn.

## Result: it churns
- 2025 list vs 2026 list: **48 of 100 names persist. 52 dropped out; 52 are new.**
- Among the 48 who persisted, the **median absolute rank move is 22 places**.
- Violent swings both ways: Dalton Caldwell 15 -> 99 · Elad Gil 34 -> 96 · Micah Rosenbloom 20 -> 71 ·
  Jordan Nof 73 -> 15 · Zach Weinberg 59 -> 9 · Ann DeWitt 90 -> 42.

## Read
A 52% one-year turnover in a ranking of the *same* population is not consistent with measuring durable
skill. What moves is which of an investor's existing bets marked up in the last twelve months — a
power-law artefact with tiny n per person. Rank is mostly a lagging indicator of recent marks.

Two-year persistence would likely be ~25%; the 2024 article is a hub page and its list could not be
extracted, so that test is open. Methodology note: the 2026 list is ranked by **Termina** (per BI's own
methodology piece), so it is a vendor model, not BI editorial judgement.

## Consequence for the FoF
Do not use it to select. Use it for three things only: (1) LP-facing credibility, (2) warm-intro
mapping, (3) sourcing the foundry — the 73 Seed-100 names absent from our graph are mostly angels
without vehicles, which is our formation pipeline, not our LP book.

This is also the template for our own scoreboard: we should publish OUR persistence, and be judged the
same way. If our manager scores churn 52% a year, they are luck too — and we should say so first.
