# Session log — 2026-08-17: the pivot from funds to people

## The decision

**Stop selecting VC funds as the product. Back individuals at their entry cheque.**
Emrah's call, and the data supports it three ways: we cannot buy the funds that measure well
(Chapter One is not raising, SV Angel/Foundation/Amplify are closed), we cannot attribute the ones
we can buy (Humba, Chemistry, Category and Theory have no scoreable history; Silent Ventures'

comparison exists the people beat the vehicle that pools them.

**Name for the product, from Emrah: "a scout fund in a box."** Sits directly above Jeremy Neilson's
"SPV in a box" from Assure. Scout programmes exist and work but are captive to the firms that run
them; nobody sells one. The four pieces are measurement, capital, rails (Sally) and back office.
Gedik's $40M stops being the business and becomes the first instance.

**Structure retained per Haluk:** $40M first close, Gedik anchoring $20M, Gedik raising $10M,

## The keystone measurement — what a tier-one arrival is actually worth

The link the whole model rested on, and it had never been measured. 15,090 companies seeded
2019-2021, outcomes run to today, split by whether a **new** top-29 firm invested within 24 months:

| | Never raised again | $50M+ | $100M+ | $250M+ | Median raised |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Drew a top-29 firm** (n=1,206) | 2.4% | 42.5% | 24.6% | 10.1% | **$37.2M** |
| Did not (n=13,884) | 26.1% | 9.6% | 4.7% | 1.5% | **$4.0M** |

**5.3x on $100M outcomes, 6.9x on $250M, 9x on median capital raised.** Predicting tier-one arrival
is predicting outcome.

**It also validated the coefficient I had flagged all session as invented.** Working the conditionals
through, an individual at 3x the base arrival rate produces a **1.50x** uplift on the chance of a
$100M outcome; `0.35 x ln(lift)` gives **1.47x**. Within 2% across the range, slightly conservative
at the top.

## Persistence, measured by walk-forward

Rates fitted on 2019-21 seed entries, scored on 2022-23, across 272 investors with enough of both.

| Fit quartile | Fit lift | Forward lift |
|---|---|---|
| Top | 2.00x | **1.95x** |
| Second | 1.37x | 1.59x |
| Third | 1.00x | 1.16x |
| Bottom | 0.60x | **0.65x** |

Monotone, threefold spread, correlation +0.30. Regression at the extreme is mild — top decile 0.82,
top twelve **0.87**. Model default moved 50% -> 85%.

**The aggregate persists; individuals do not.** Inside that same top twelve, SV Angel went
3.31x -> 5.28x while Naval Ravikant went 2.35x -> **zero** on 29 bets, and Ameet Patel 2.84x -> zero
on 10. That dispersion is the empirical case for a bench rather than one person, and for keeping
estimation error in the model.

## Two design errors caught and corrected

**1. SPV-into-follow-ons buys the brand, not the edge.** Sizing a programme off Etched's Series C
means paying to follow someone into a price they already made. The edge was entering at seed. So the
fund backs the **entry cheque** — each person gets an annual allocation to deploy at their own
discretion at seed, keeps most of the carry, pays no management fee, and we hold final approval.

**2. The SPV "audition phase" does not do what I claimed.** SPV outcomes do not resolve in 18 months;
the picking is verifiable from records now, for free. Worse, funding SPVs first teaches the
counterparty that our capital arrives without GP economics attached. Its real uses are narrower:
access in the meantime, and letting *them* get comfortable with *us*.

**Economics for a Mei-type deal:** SPV carry is small money ($400k pool on a $1M cheque at 3x); a
$25M Fund I on 2/20 is ~$15M of GP economics, so a 15% stake is $2.2M from one manager. Give away
the SPV carry entirely to buy the right to anchor the fund. Step the GP stake down 20/15/10 across
Funds I-III.

## Capacity is the question that decides everything

Can a $25k invitation become a $250k allocation? A founder lets an angel in for $25k for their name;
$250k competes with the lead. If no, the structure caps below fund size. **Answered by asking, and
it should be the first question in every conversation** — before track record, before terms. Sharper
version noted late: a Sequoia scout gets allocation partly *because it is Sequoia*, so this is a
question about our brand as much as theirs.

## The moat claim I made and had to withdraw

I claimed nobody else could produce this ranking. **False, and Emrah caught it.** Any PitchBook or
Crunchbase seat plus a week of analyst time reproduces it; Dealroom, Harmonic and Specter sell the
data; SignalFire and Correlation are built on this kind of analysis. **AngelList's version would be
strictly better** — they observe realised angel returns, not a follow-on proxy, and already have the
relationships. The defensible claim is *"we did this first and are set up to act on it"*, not
*"only we can see this."* Nothing on the page claims exclusivity; the overclaim was conversational.

## Pipeline defect (unfixed)

content.** Farzad Salim: 26 pulls across 22 days, zero content changes — likely why he keeps
surfacing in Emrah's LinkedIn feed. Concentrated in watchlists, not corpus-wide: a random sample

`(url, date)` plus a hash short-circuit; de-duplicated, the collection becomes a de-stealth detector.

and health; blind to consumer, fintech and defence.
